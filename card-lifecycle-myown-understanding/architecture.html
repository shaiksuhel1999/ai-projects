The four-party model

An authorization crosses four institutions in about a second

A card is an instrument on a revolving credit account. When it is tapped, the request hops from the terminal to the acquiring bank, across the card network, to the issuer that actually holds the money — and the decision travels the same road back.

Clearing & settlement

Approval is a promise; posting is the money

An approved authorization is only a hold. The merchant captures it (clearing), a batch job settles it between banks, and only then is the amount truly posted. Every branch off the happy path is its own terminal state.

The money model

Two balances: what’s posted, and what’s pending

A real card ledger separates the posted balance (hard-posted, drives the statement) from the memo balance (pending entries that already affect what you can spend). Available credit is computed from both, plus authorization holds.

availableCredit = creditLimit −postedBalance −holdAmount −memoBalance

A memo debit is positive and shrinks available credit; a memo credit (like a dispute’s provisional credit) is negative and frees it up.

Disputes & chargebacks

A chargeback is a small state machine with money attached

When a cardholder disputes a posted purchase, the issuer can grant a provisional credit while it investigates. The merchant re-presents evidence; the case is won or lost — and the provisional credit is kept or clawed back accordingly.

Billing · payments · lending

The cycle that turns spending into a statement

At the cut date the billing engine sweeps the cycle, charges interest and fees, and computes the minimum due. Purchases can convert to EMIs; personal loans are underwritten and amortized on a reducing-balance schedule.

  1. Cut the statement — opening balance, purchases, cash advances, fees, interest, credits → closing balance.
  2. Revolving interest & late fee — charged when the prior statement wasn’t paid in full or the minimum was missed.
  3. Minimum due — the greater of a percentage of the balance or a floor, capped at the balance.
  4. Pay — minimum, total, or a custom amount; autopay can do this automatically on the due date.
  5. Lend — convert a purchase to a fixed EMI plan, or take a risk-priced personal loan with a full amortization schedule.
